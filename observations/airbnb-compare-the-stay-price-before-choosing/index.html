Airbnb: Compare the stay price before choosing

A nightly headline is not enough to compare stays when fees vary. Airbnb's announcement introduced a total-price display before booking. The design decision is where to reveal the amount used for comparison, not simply how large to make the price text.

Captured demo: results labeled $1,212 total before taxes, then trip summary/breakdown with $1,212.20 plus $96.97 taxes for $1,309.17. The selected earlier frame already has the toggle on, so this is not an observed off/on A/B comparison. These are one historical demo booking's numbers, not rates. This shows one booking's labels and breakdown, not successful comparison across multiple stays.

Visible: the result card shows a stay total labeled "before taxes." Sourced: Airbnb presented this as a change from nightly-price-led comparison. The selected frames do not show the off/on transition.

Visible: "before taxes" names an exclusion. Inference: explicit exclusion may help comprehension, but the displayed comparison number still is not the final checkout amount.

The breakdown explains the final amount rather than presenting a new unexplained total. Inference: this may improve price comprehension, not necessarily conversion.

Hypothesized risks and unobserved states: Pre-tax comparison can still surprise someone who reads total as all-in. Search dates, guest counts, currency, discounts and refundable-rate differences must stay comparable. Booking confirmation, cancellation policy, refund rules and later default changes are outside this recording.

What to measure
  1. 01Ask users to compare two stays and predict their final payable amount before opening checkout.
  2. 02Track prediction error and price-surprise abandonment, with cancellation/refund understanding as a guardrail.
  3. 03Do not claim reduced fees from a change in fee visibility.
Job to be done

Assumed scenario; no independent user research. Traveler comparing dated stays with different fees, wanting comparable payable amounts; workaround: open each checkout. Company-announced price-visibility problem.

Alternative considered

Always-on all-in totals including tax where feasible, with a breakdown. The historical toggle/pre-tax approach wins only if it balances comparability and correct tax context. Any comparison must hold dates, guest count, currency and applicable discounts constant.

What would reverse this

Decision would shift toward the alternative if: People still expect the pre-tax number to be final, or all-in totals give more accurate comparisons.

Test scope

Exploratory protocol: recruit people who have the stated job, randomize prototype order against the alternative, and measure successful tasks out of eligible attempts in one session. Follow-up recall, attendance, payment reconciliation or delivery outcomes require a separate real-use study. No sample size, target lift or causal result is claimed.

Choose a consistent comparison unit and name what it still excludes.
