Duolingo: Make the next lesson obvious

The company says learners asked whether they were using Duolingo in the best way. The old tree shows several selectable topics and crown levels. Our hypothesis: course sequencing can become work the learner must do before learning.

Visible: a highlighted START node sits after completed checks and before locked nodes. A second composite places a Story in the same path. Lesson completion and the return transition are not shown.

One highlighted next action narrows the immediate choice. Completed and future nodes communicate progress, not simply a score.

The unit heading names an action, such as greeting people. The guidebook sits nearby as optional explanation.

Sourced: the company describes interleaving skills for spaced practice and moving Stories into the path. Inference: this may reduce activity-selection work. Screens do not prove learning efficacy.

Hypothesized risks and unobserved states: A default curriculum takes control away from learners who want a particular topic or quick revision. The launch FAQ documents replay and a floating return arrow; these are sourced escape routes, not shown here. Placement, locked-node taps, lesson completion and resuming after time away remain unaudited.

What to measure
  1. 01Compare next-lesson start latency and abandonment, alongside delayed recall and performance on unfamiliar examples.
  2. 02Segment returning learners and track time to find targeted review.
  3. 03Faster starts with worse recall are not success.
Job to be done

Assumed scenario; no independent user research. Beginner returning to course home, wanting a useful next practice without designing a curriculum; workaround: choose topics from a tree. Company-documented concern, inferred planning cost.

Alternative considered

Keep the skill tree and add a recommended-next card using the same interleaved curriculum. It preserves browsing while recommending the same next lesson; the path wins only if its sequence context outweighs reduced topic access.

What would reverse this

Decision would shift toward the alternative if: A recommended card yields the same starts and recall while targeted revision is easier.

Test scope

Exploratory protocol: recruit people who have the stated job, randomize prototype order against the alternative, and measure successful tasks out of eligible attempts in one session. Follow-up recall, attendance, payment reconciliation or delivery outcomes require a separate real-use study. No sample size, target lift or causal result is claimed.

Remove planning work only when the default embodies a defensible plan and users can recover control.
