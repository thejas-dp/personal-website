Google Pay: Offer a route for the identifier in hand

People may know a phone number, UPI ID, bank account or QR code, rather than the recipient name in an address book. A payment entry screen needs to bridge those identifiers without treating an identifier as proof of the right recipient.

Historical screen: Bills & Transfers groups Mobile recharge, Bank transfer, Bill payments, UPI ID or QR, Phone number and Self Transfer. The adjacent Business tab separates merchant discovery. Current official help documents search by name, phone/UPI number, VPA, bank account/IFSC and QR. It also documents recipient selection, Pay, amount, Proceed and UPI PIN. These later steps are sourced, not screenshot-observed.

Visible: separate controls include phone number and UPI ID/QR. Our reading is that these routes align with identifiers users may hold. Inference: that may reduce translation into app categories.

The historical screen separates recipient-transfer routes from merchant discovery. The tabs and quick actions are not claimed as today's layout.

Official help separates selecting a receiver from entering the amount and authorizing with a PIN. The shown entry screen cannot prove adequate receiver verification at that later boundary.

Hypothesized risks and unobserved states: Multiple routes can make the first screen busier and can yield the same receiver through several paths. Reassuring visual familiarity does not establish recipient correctness. No-match, reused phone numbers, wrong-recipient detection, bank selection, pending payments and retry safety are not visible. A failed or stuck payment should never be equated with safe permission to pay again.

What to measure
  1. 01Test identifier-to-receiver task completion across new and repeat receivers.
  2. 02At review, ask people to identify receiver and amount correctly.
  3. 03Guard against wrong-recipient payments and duplicate retries; do not optimize PIN-step speed as an isolated conversion metric.
Job to be done

Assumed scenario; no independent user research. Payer holding a phone number, VPA or QR, wanting the right receiver rather than a category tour; workaround: copy identifier into search. Documented supported identifiers, inferred entry burden.

Alternative considered

A single identifier-detecting search field plus QR is a historical-screen comparison concept, not a claim that today's Google Pay lacks it. Current help already documents unified search. Distinct entry routes win only if they reduce identifier confusion more than they add menu complexity.

What would reverse this

Decision would shift toward the alternative if: Unified search works equally well, or route familiarity increases wrong-receiver confidence. This tests the rationale of the 2020 entry screen, not a recommendation to replace current Google Pay.

Test scope

Exploratory protocol: recruit people who have the stated job, randomize prototype order against the alternative, and measure successful tasks out of eligible attempts in one session. Follow-up recall, attendance, payment reconciliation or delivery outcomes require a separate real-use study. No sample size, target lift or causal result is claimed.

Use familiar identifiers for entry, but spend deliberate attention at identity and money boundaries.
