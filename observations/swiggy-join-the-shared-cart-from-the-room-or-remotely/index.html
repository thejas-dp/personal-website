Swiggy: Join the shared cart from the room or remotely

Guests sitting nearby and people coordinating remotely need different invitation routes. The official engineering account describes expanding an early same-network proof of concept to cross-platform and remote participation, with QR codes and links.

Visible archive capture: invitation screen with QR code, Continue to group order and Share a link instead. The expandable QR provides a same-room route; sharing provides a remote route. Guest joining, permissions, cart editing and checkout are not visible in this capture.

QR and link sharing match two physical contexts without requiring the host to manually enter every guest.

Continue to group order gives the host a route back to the task instead of trapping them on the invitation step.

Sourced: Swiggy's engineers describe synchronization, host editing and scalability problems in development. Those details show operational stakes; they do not prove every released edge case is resolved.

Hypothesized risks and unobserved states: A shareable join link can spread beyond the intended group. Expiry, revocation, guest identity, late joining and access after checkout need explicit rules. The archive does not expose its capture date, so this is a narrow invitation-pattern study, not a dated whole-flow audit or a current-limit claim.

What to measure
  1. 01Compare guest-join completion for same-room QR and remote link sharing.
  2. 02Measure wrong-group joins and time for hosts to regain the cart.
  3. 03Stress-test concurrent edits and inventory changes separately; invitation conversion alone can hide a broken shared cart.
Job to be done

Assumed scenario; no independent user research. Host inviting guests in the room and remotely, wanting everyone in the same intended cart; workaround: relay meal choices manually. Swiggy's engineering article describes expanding a same-network prototype to remote/cross-platform participation; the screenshot is from third-party Mobbin.

Alternative considered

A link-first invitation with QR as a secondary reveal could serve both contexts. The current QR-first modal wins only if same-room joining is easier without making remote sharing or access rules harder to find.

What would reverse this

Decision would shift toward the alternative if: Wrong-group joins rise, or link expiry/revocation is unclear enough to make sharing unsafe.

Test scope

Exploratory protocol: recruit people who have the stated job, randomize prototype order against the alternative, and measure successful tasks out of eligible attempts in one session. Follow-up recall, attendance, payment reconciliation or delivery outcomes require a separate real-use study. No sample size, target lift or causal result is claimed.

Choose the handoff around the real context, then verify the shared state behind it.
