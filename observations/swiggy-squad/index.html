Swiggy / Group Ordering

Design concept. This is a proposed idea, not a claim that the product offers it.

Swiggy Squad

Collaborative ordering distributes decision-making across the entire group.

Group food ordering is a solved problem that nobody has actually solved. Every time a group of people tries to order together it goes the same way — someone starts a WhatsApp thread, everyone sends their order in different formats, one person consolidates it, places the order, and then chases everyone for money afterward. It works, but it is entirely held together by one person's patience and a payment app.

Squad makes the coordination native to Swiggy. You create a group — House Party, Office, Home, College batch — members join, everyone browses and adds to a shared cart simultaneously, and a single checkout covers the whole order. No consolidation thread. No one person carrying the cognitive load. Four people adding items from four different restaurants, one order, multiple delivery partners converging at the same location.

The multi-restaurant part is the genuinely hard product problem. It is not just a shared cart — it is coordinated logistics across different kitchens and delivery partners arriving at the same drop point within a reasonable time window. The Squad Delivery view shows exactly this: three delivery partners in transit, individual ETAs, a live map. The complexity is real and the UI has to make it feel manageable rather than chaotic.

The social layer compounds the utility. Squads persist — they are not one-time sessions. Your Office squad has 14 orders of history. Your House Party group has 8 members. The group becomes a standing ordering context, which means every future group meal starts from a shared history rather than from scratch.

The friction in group ordering was never about the food. It was about the coordination tax one person always paid on everyone else's behalf. Squad distributes that tax across the group and makes Swiggy the coordinator instead of the most organised person in the chat.
