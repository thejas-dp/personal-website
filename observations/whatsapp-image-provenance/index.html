WhatsApp Image Provenance

Image provenance helps users trace where forwarded media originally came from.

The most dangerous misinformation in India doesn't travel through Twitter or news sites. It travels through family WhatsApp groups, as a forwarded image, from someone you trust.

The problem with forwarded misinformation is that the social wrapper makes it feel credible. It came from your uncle. Your uncle got it from someone he trusts. By the time it reaches you it has accumulated a chain of implicit endorsements from real people, which is exactly what makes it so effective and so hard to counter.

Image Provenance puts the metadata where the image is. Not in a fact-checking tab, not behind a link, but as a native overlay on the image itself — forwarded 1,400 times, first seen on WhatsApp 8 months ago. Two data points. No editorial judgment. No label saying true or false. Just the raw provenance, surfaced at the moment of viewing.

Those two numbers do more than any warning label could. A flood image being forwarded as breaking news that was first seen 8 months ago is not breaking news. A claim forwarded 1,400 times with no origin date pattern reads differently from one forwarded 12 times from a recent source. The user draws their own conclusion — which means they actually believe it rather than dismissing it as a platform telling them what to think.

Misinformation interventions fail when they're adversarial — when the platform tells the user they're wrong, the user gets defensive. This works because it's just information about the image, not a judgment about the person who sent it. The nudge lands softly, in the right place, at the right moment, and lets the user do the thinking themselves.
