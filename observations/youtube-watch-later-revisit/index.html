YouTube Watch Later Revisit

Watch Later resurfacing prompts users to revisit content they intended to return to.

Everyone's Watch Later is a lie they told themselves. 47 videos saved, zero watched, oldest from 8 months ago. The intent was real at the moment of saving. The follow-through never came.

The problem is not that people don't want to watch the videos. It's that Watch Later has no re-entry mechanism. You save something, it disappears into a list, and the next time you open YouTube the algorithm serves you something new and more immediately compelling. The saved video never gets a second chance to surface.

Watch Later Revisit fixes this with a single card in the feed — a gentle, slightly self-aware nudge that says you saved 47 videos and haven't watched any of them. Not a notification, not a separate tab, but a native feed card that meets the user exactly where they already are. From there, two options: clear the guilt in one tap, or actually watch something now.

The mechanic doing the real work is re-surfacing at the right moment. Not when the user navigates to their library. Not via a push notification they'll dismiss. Inside the feed, during the exact session where they were about to let the algorithm decide for them. That timing is everything.

Watch Later fails not as a saving feature but as a watching feature. The save is frictionless. The retrieval is dead. This card is just a retrieval mechanism that doesn't require the user to remember they ever saved anything — which is the only kind that actually works.
