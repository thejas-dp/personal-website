Zomato: Split meal choice from order ownership

One person collecting everyone's preferences becomes the bottleneck for a group meal. Group ordering lets participants choose their own items while a host coordinates the combined order. This problem framing is illustrative; the screenshot does not measure host workload.

The reproduced launch composite shows the restaurant Group Order entry, group setup and participant cart. Setup text shows up to 30 friends, a 30-minute window and host pays. Those are source-date settings, not a promise about current limits. Payment, invitation acceptance and simultaneous editing are not recorded.

The entry is tied to the restaurant rather than a separate social area. Inference: it keeps collaboration inside the order task.

Setup communicates participation and payment ownership before the cart is shared. Independent item choice is not independent checkout.

Visible: the reproduced cart shows named participant sections (Deepi, Manas, Vikram and Niharika). Inference: attribution may help the host reconcile requests. This does not establish conflict handling or synchronization.

Hypothesized risks and unobserved states: A host paying creates reimbursement and responsibility questions. A deadline may help closure but exclude late participants. Item availability changes, price updates, dietary mistakes, guest removal, abandoned carts and payment failures are unobserved. Do not turn source settings into evidence that these edge cases are solved.

What to measure
  1. 01Test time to assemble an accurate group cart, host correction effort and participant completion.
  2. 02Guard against unapproved items, unnoticed late edits and total-price surprises.
  3. 03Average order value is a business result, not proof of better coordination.
Job to be done

Assumed scenario; no independent user research. Meal organizer with several participants at one restaurant, wanting a correct combined cart; workaround: collect messages and type every item. Hypothesized coordination cost.

Alternative considered

Host-owned cart with a shared text list. Shared editing wins only if fewer transcription mistakes outweigh synchronization and ownership problems.

What would reverse this

Decision would shift toward the alternative if: Host corrections or unapproved items rise, or the simple list is equally accurate and faster.

Test scope

Exploratory protocol: recruit people who have the stated job, randomize prototype order against the alternative, and measure successful tasks out of eligible attempts in one session. Follow-up recall, attendance, payment reconciliation or delivery outcomes require a separate real-use study. No sample size, target lift or causal result is claimed.

Distribute selection without hiding who owns the final commitment.
