Product metrics tree

Metrics tree: Spotify

A framework exercise. Metric definitions are mine. No Spotify internal numbers are used or implied.

Business model first

Spotify earns from two engines: Premium subscriptions (users pay) and ad-supported free (advertisers pay for attention). Both depend on the same asset: people returning to listen. So the tree has to connect listening habit to both revenue lines.

North Star

Weekly engaged listeners: unique users who listen for at least a minimum threshold of minutes on at least a minimum number of days in a week.

Why not total streams or total hours? Those reward a few heavy listeners and hide a leaking base. Why not MAU? It counts a person who opened the app once. Engagement that is habitual predicts both conversion to Premium and ad revenue.

The tree

Weekly engaged listeners
├── Acquisition: new users reaching the app
│   ├── Installs / signups by channel (organic, referral, partner, paid)
│   ├── Signup completion rate
│   └── Cost per activated user (guardrail)
├── Activation: first-session value
│   ├── % of new users who play something within first session
│   ├── % who follow/save/create something in week 1 (taste signal captured)
│   └── Time to first "good" listen (skip rate low, completion high)
├── Engagement: habit depth
│   ├── Listening days per week
│   ├── Minutes per session and sessions per day
│   ├── Share of listening from personalised surfaces (Discover Weekly, Daily Mix, Home)
│   └── Content breadth: genres, formats (music, podcast, audiobook)
├── Retention: staying
│   ├── Week 1, week 4, week 12 return rates by cohort
│   ├── Resurrection rate of lapsed users
│   └── Churn of Premium subscribers (monthly)
└── Monetization (health check, not the north star)
    ├── Free to Premium conversion
    ├── Premium ARPU and plan mix (individual, family, student)
    └── Ad revenue per free listening hour

Reading the tree

  • Acquisition feeds activation, activation feeds engagement. A big acquisition number with weak activation is a leaky bucket and it shows up as poor week 4 retention.
  • Taste capture in week 1 is the hidden lever. Follows, saves and playlists give the recommender something to work with. Fewer signals means generic recommendations means weaker habit.
  • Personalised-surface share is a quality signal. If a growing share of listening comes from recommendation surfaces and satisfaction holds, the recommender is doing its job.

Counter-metrics and guardrails

  • Skip rate and "thumbs down" on recommendations (are we engaging or just nagging?)
  • Notification opt-out rate (are re-engagement pushes burning goodwill?)
  • Ad load per hour on free tier vs retention of free users
  • Premium churn after price changes

How a PM would use it

  1. Diagnose: if the North Star dips, walk down the tree. Did acquisition fall, activation, or retention? Segment by cohort and platform.
  2. Prioritise: choose the branch with the biggest gap between current and plausible, and the most direct lever.
  3. Instrument: each node needs one owner team and a definition that doesn't change quietly.

Example diagnosis

Suppose weekly engaged listeners are flat but new signups are up. The tree says check activation and week 4 retention first, specifically whether new users are capturing taste signal in week 1. Hypothesis to test: a lighter onboarding taste picker raises week 1 saves, which raises week 4 return.

Takeaway

One north star, five branches, a guardrail on each. If a team can't point to its node, the metric system isn't working.
