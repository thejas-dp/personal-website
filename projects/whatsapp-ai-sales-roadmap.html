Product roadmap

6-12 month roadmap: a WhatsApp-first AI sales assistant for Indian D2C brands

A roadmap exercise for a hypothetical seed-stage startup. All strategy is reasoned, not measured. No traction numbers are assumed.

The company (hypothetical)

Small team, pre-seed or seed, selling to Indian D2C brands that already get leads and customer questions on WhatsApp. The product: an AI assistant that answers product questions, recovers abandoned carts, and hands off to humans when needed.

Why WhatsApp

It's where Indian customers already talk to brands. Brands handle it manually or with rigid bots. An AI agent that works inside existing conversations has a clear job: answer fast, sell, hand off cleanly.

Strategy in one line

Earn trust with one narrow, provable use case, then widen. Early-stage roadmaps fail by being a list of features. This one is a sequence of bets, each unlocking the next.

Prioritisation approach

Every item is scored on: impact on the one metric that matters now, confidence, effort, and whether it unblocks later work. The north star for the first year: conversations resolved or converted without human help, at an acceptable correction rate.

Roadmap

Phase 1 (months 0 to 3): Make one use case work

Item Why now
Product Q&A from the brand's catalog and policies Highest frequency question type for most D2C brands, easy to verify
Human handoff with full context Trust requires an escape hatch. Without it brands won't turn it on
Conversation log with corrections Our learning loop. Every fix teaches us where the agent fails
5 design-partner brands, weekly calls Early signal beats features

Deliberately not built: analytics dashboards, multi-language, broadcast campaigns.

Phase 2 (months 3 to 6): Prove it sells

Item Why now
Abandoned cart and browse recovery over WhatsApp Directly tied to revenue, so ROI is visible
Order status and returns handling Removes the biggest support load, builds repeat trust
Guardrails: approved claims, price/discount limits Brands will not risk off-brand or wrong-price messages
Simple outcomes report (conversations, handoffs, recovered carts) The renewal conversation depends on it

Phase 3 (months 6 to 9): Make it self-serve and safer

Item Why now
Self-serve onboarding with catalog import and preview Founders can't hand-onboard every brand beyond the first handful
Evaluation set from real conversations Lets us ship model/prompt changes without regressions
Shopify and one more commerce platform integration Reduces setup friction, widens addressable brands

Phase 4 (months 9 to 12): Widen

Item Why now
Vernacular language support Valuable, but only after core quality is proven in one language
Proactive flows (reorder reminders, post-purchase) Needs trust built in Phases 1 to 3
Team inbox features and role permissions Demanded by growing brands, not before

Dependencies and sequencing logic

  • Handoff and correction logging come before cart recovery, because outbound selling raises the stakes of mistakes.
  • Evaluation set comes before language expansion, because each new language multiplies the surface to regress.
  • Self-serve comes only after the first 5 to 10 brands show the use case works.

Metrics by phase

  • P1: share of questions answered without handoff; correction rate on reviewed conversations
  • P2: recovered cart revenue as reported by brands; handoff rate
  • P3: time to go live for a new brand; weekly active brands
  • P4: retention of brands after 90 days; expansion usage

Risks and what would change the plan

  • Messaging platform policy and cost changes could squeeze margins. Watch pricing per conversation and design for fewer, higher-value messages.
  • If brands won't give catalog data cleanly, move integration work earlier.
  • If correction rates stay high in P1, do not proceed to outbound selling.

Takeaway

A good early roadmap says no loudly. Each phase exists to earn the right to build the next one.
