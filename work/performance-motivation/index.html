PRODUCT CASE STUDY

Performance Powered Motivation System

Making incentives transparent and actionable for InPrime's field sales force

Founding Member — Product & Growth, Niti AI · Discovery, product direction, UX, delivery with the team
PPMS
Incentive transparency for frontline sales

The Problem

InPrime, an SME lender and one of Niti AI's first paying customers, came with a business problem, not a product spec: field sales staff were dropping off after 3–6 months. The incentive structure was complex, changed frequently, and lived scattered across Excel sheets and managers' WhatsApp messages, consolidated only at month end. A salesperson mid-month couldn't see what they had earned or what to do to earn more. This isn't an InPrime-specific quirk — in Indian NBFC field sales, attrition runs 70–96% a year, and 74% of sales reps say they don't trust how their pay is calculated. Reps who can't see what they earn are the ones who leave.

Discovery

I owned discovery end to end: sat with roughly 10–20 salespeople to understand how they experienced incentives day to day, worked through the numbers with accounting and finance, and studied how market incentive systems are structured. The hypothesis that came out of it: making incentives transparent and actionable — visible in the moment, not at month end — could improve performance.

What We Built

Around eight user flows that turned the incentive scheme from a month-end surprise into a daily tool: live earnings visibility, potential earnings (what you would take home at your current pace, and what more is within reach), actionable guidance on which activities move earnings, leaderboards, and recognition. I set the product direction, contributed the UX, and worked with the team to ship it.

The test of whether it mattered: during a 3-day takedown, 80+ of roughly 250 salespeople asked where it went. That's a demand signal, not proof of retention — but people don't miss tools they never used.

What It Proved

Start from the business problem, not the customer's proposed solution. InPrime didn't ask for an incentive-transparency product — they described attrition and inconsistency, and the product followed from discovery. Hypothesis first, then delivery, then evidence of value. It's the way I like to work, and the project I'm most proud of.
